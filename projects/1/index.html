Project 1: Prokudin-Gorskii Colorization

← Back to Portfolio

Overview

In this project, I used digitized glass plates — taken by Prokudin-Gorskii in the early 1900s by using red, green, and blue filters for a total of three exposures for each photograph — to reconstruct color images through careful alignment of the three color channels. First, I split the glass plate scan into three sections to separate each color filter exposure. To align the images, the code searches over a small window (for my implementation, 15 pixels wide/tall) and evaluates how well the color channels match with Normalized Cross-Correlation. This kind of exhaustive search works well enough for .jpg files, but larger .tif files required a more efficient, image pyramid method (going from small, scaled-down, coarse/blurry images to the full-resolution starting image and shifting the alignment at each level) in order to run in a reasonable amount of time. Then, to improve accuracy even further, I implemented edge detection and used that as the feature space for scoring via NCC rather than simply RGB values.

Method / Approach

  1. Single-scale baseline:

    For small JPGs, I implemented an exhaustive integer search within a fixed window (e.g., ±15 pixels) using Zero-mean Normalized Cross-Correlation (ZNCC). This works well enough for low-resolution images but is too slow and limited for larger .tif files.

  2. Image preprocessing & cropping:

    Each plate is read, converted to float64 in [0,1], and split into B, G, and R channels. I applied a hard pre-crop (to remove thick borders), ensuring the wraparound edges and black borders don’t bias the metrics. Then, I also applied an interior crop when scoring with ZNCC to avoid wraparound artifacts caused by np.roll (and to utilize the center of the image, which is most reliable due to vignetting, chromatic aberration, etc.)

  3. Pyramid (multi-scale) approach:

    For high-resolution .tif files, I downscale the channels repeatedly by 0.5 until the smallest side is ~250 px. Alignment starts at the coarsest level with a slightly larger search window (10x10 pixels), then at each finer level the shift is doubled and refined locally (5x5 pixels). This coarse-to-fine refinement allows handling of large displacements efficiently while keeping runtime reasonable.

  4. Edge detection:

    To mitigate rainbow ghosting in textured or low-contrast regions, I added an edge-based mode where ZNCC is computed not on raw intensity but on edge maps. I implemented Sobel, Scharr, and Gaussian gradient magnitude. This makes alignment more robust, especially on images with strong borders or repeated patterns (e.g., melons).

Issues & Fixes

  • Rainbow ghosting: reduced with stricter cropping and edge-based scoring.
  • Large shifts in TIFFs: handled via coarse→fine image pyramid.

Single-Scale Alignment

Approach

Exhaustive search over ±window on the original resolution using NCC. Scores are computed on an interior crop (cutting off 10% on each side, so essentially about 64% of the image) to avoid border artifacts. Offsets are reported as (dx, dy) with +dx = right and +dy = down.

Multi-Scale Pyramid Alignment (with NCC)

Step-by-step

  1. Build pyramid by downscaling by 0.5 until min side ≈ 250 px.
  2. At the coarsest level, search a slightly larger window (e.g., ±10) with NCC; record best (dx, dy).
  3. Move one level finer: double (dx, dy) and refine in a small window (±5).
  4. Repeat until full resolution; apply final shifts to compose RGB.

Difficult / Failure Cases

emir.tif (challenging without edge detection): The Emir’s portrait has strong, saturated reds and dark blues in the robe and background. These channels differ substantially in brightness and contrast, so raw-intensity NCC tends to maximize correlation on color rather than structure. In addition, the fabric’s ornate, repeating patterns produce many near-duplicate local patches. Together, these effects create false peaks in the NCC surface and encourage small but visible misalignments (color fringing), especially around the robe trim and high-contrast borders.

Mitigation: using Sobel/Scharr edge maps for scoring makes the metric far less sensitive to per-channel brightness/saturation differences and repetitive color textures. Combined with a hard pre-crop and a slightly larger coarse-level search window in the pyramid, the edge-based NCC reliably snaps the channels to structural boundaries (seams, facial contours, and jewelry edges), removing the rainbow halo.
