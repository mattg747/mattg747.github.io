Project 1: Prokudin-Gorskii Colorization

← Back to Portfolio

Overview

In this project, I used digitized glass plates — taken by Prokudin-Gorskii in the early 1900s by using red, green, and blue filters for a total of three exposures for each photograph — to reconstruct color images through careful alignment of the three color channels. First, I split the glass plate scan into three sections to separate each color filter exposure. To align the images, the code searches over a small window (for my implementation, 15 pixels wide/tall) and evaluates how well the color channels match with Normalized Cross-Correlation. This kind of exhaustive search works well enough for .jpg files, but larger .tif files required a more efficient, image pyramid method (going from small, scaled-down, coarse/blurry images to the full-resolution starting image and shifting the alignment at each level) in order to run in a reasonable amount of time. Then, to improve accuracy even further, I implemented edge detection and used that as the feature space for scoring via NCC rather than simply RGB values.

Method / Approach

  1. Single-scale baseline:

    For small JPGs, I implemented an exhaustive integer search within a fixed window (e.g., ±15 pixels) using Zero-mean Normalized Cross-Correlation (ZNCC). This works well enough for low-resolution images but is too slow and limited for larger .tif files.

  2. Image preprocessing & cropping:

    Each plate is read, converted to float64 in [0,1], and split into B, G, and R channels. I applied a hard pre-crop to remove thick borders, plus an interior crop during scoring to avoid wraparound artifacts from np.roll.

  3. Pyramid (multi-scale) approach:

    For high-resolution .tif files, I downscale the channels repeatedly by 0.5 until the smallest side is ~250 px. Alignment starts at the coarsest level with a larger window, then refines at each finer level.

  4. Edge detection:

    To mitigate rainbow ghosting in textured or low-contrast regions, I compute ZNCC on edge maps (Sobel, Scharr, or Gaussian gradient magnitude) instead of raw intensity.

Issues & Fixes

  • Rainbow ghosting: reduced with stricter cropping and edge-based scoring.
  • Large shifts in TIFFs: handled via coarse→fine image pyramid.

Single-Scale Alignment

Approach

Exhaustive search over ±window on the original resolution using NCC. Scores are computed on an interior crop (cutting off 10% on each side) to avoid border artifacts. Offsets are reported as (dx, dy) with +dx = right and +dy = down.

Multi-Scale Pyramid Alignment (with NCC)

Step-by-step

  1. Build pyramid by downscaling by 0.5 until min side ≈ 250 px.
  2. Coarsest level: search ±10 with NCC, record (dx, dy).
  3. At each finer level: double the shift and refine in ±5.
  4. At full resolution: apply final shifts to compose RGB.

Difficult / Failure Cases

emir.tif (challenging without edge detection): The Emir’s portrait has strong, saturated reds and dark blues in the robe and background. These channels differ in brightness and contrast, so raw-intensity NCC tends to maximize correlation on color rather than structure. The fabric’s ornate, repeating patterns create many near-duplicate patches, leading to false peaks and small but visible misalignments around the robe trim and borders.

Mitigation: scoring on Sobel/Scharr edge maps reduces sensitivity to per-channel brightness/saturation and to repetitive color textures. With a hard pre-crop and a slightly larger coarse search window, edge-based NCC snaps channels to structural boundaries (seams, contours, jewelry edges), removing the rainbow halo.
