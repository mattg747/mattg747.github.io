Overview

This project explores 2D filtering in both the spatial and frequency domains. In Part 1 I implement convolution from scratch and compare finite differences to derivative-of-Gaussian (DoG) filters for edge detection. In Part 2 I apply those ideas to unsharp masking (image sharpening), hybrid images that change interpretation with distance, and multiresolution blending using Gaussian/Laplacian stacks for seamless composites.

  • Languages / libs: Python, NumPy, SciPy, OpenCV, Matplotlib
  • Concepts: convolution & padding, finite differences, Gaussian smoothing, DoG, unsharp mask, frequency analysis (FFT), Gaussian/Laplacian stacks, multi-resolution blending

I emphasize the trade-off between noise suppression and detail preservation, and show how operating at different frequency bands explains both edge quality and why multires blending removes seams.

Part 1.1 — Convolutions from Scratch

I wrote 2D convolution with a four-loop reference and a faster two-loop vectorized version (NumPy only). Padding is zero-filled. I validate against scipy.signal.convolve2d and discuss boundary handling and runtime.

Notes on padding & runtime

  • Padding: zero padding slightly darkens borders compared to reflect/symmetric. I match SciPy by using the same mode.
  • Runtime: the two-loop version is ~10–30× faster than four-loop on ~1 MP images (depends on kernel size).
  • Sanity: box filter blurs uniformly; Dx/Dy respond at vertical/horizontal edges and flip sign with contrast.

Part 1.2 — Finite Difference Operator

I estimate partial derivatives with Dx = [-1, +1] and Dy = [-1; +1]. The gradient magnitude is ‖∇I‖ = √(Ix² + Iy²). To binarize edges I sweep a threshold and pick one that keeps structural contours (tripod, jacket outline) while suppressing noise.

  • Observation: raw finite differences are sensitive to noise—fine textures show up as false edges.
  • Choice: I report a threshold near T ≈ 0.20 on [0,1] images, which balanced completeness vs. noise.

Part 1.3 — Gaussian & Derivative of Gaussian (DoG)

I build 2D Gaussians using cv2.getGaussianKernel and form DoG filters by convolving the Gaussian with the finite-difference operators. I compare (a) blur → finite differences against (b) single-pass DoG.

Takeaway

  • Blur→FD suppresses noise then differentiates — good but still leaks textured noise.
  • DoG bakes smoothing into the derivative and gives crisper structural edges with fewer false positives.

Part 2.3 — Gaussian & Laplacian Stacks (Apple & Orange)

Stacks (no downsampling): each Gaussian level further blurs the previous; each Laplacian level is the band-pass difference between consecutive Gaussians. Reconstruction ≈ sum of Laplacians + coarsest Gaussian.

Takeaways

  • Filtering = frequency sculpting: Gaussian removes highs; DoG detects edges while suppressing noise.
  • Sharpening = controlled boost of highs: α controls “crispy vs. crunchy.” Too large amplifies noise/halos.
  • Hybrids: perception flips with viewing distance because the visual system attends to different bands.
  • Stacks matter: blending at multiple bands lets seams fade naturally from coarse→fine detail.
